Reconstitution for lab work

How laboratories reconstitute lyophilized material: choosing a diluent, aseptic technique and working out concentration.

Start with the written protocol

Reconstitution should follow your institution’s written protocol and the documentation for the lot. This guide describes general laboratory practice for in-vitro work only. It does not describe use in people or animals.

Choosing a diluent

Common laboratory diluents include sterile water and bacteriostatic water. The right choice depends on the assay: a preservative such as benzyl alcohol can interfere with some cell-based work. See the comparison guide on bacteriostatic and sterile water.

Aseptic technique

Let the vial reach room temperature, wipe the stopper with alcohol, use sterile transfer equipment, and add diluent slowly down the vial wall so the powder dissolves without foaming. Swirl gently rather than shaking.

Working out concentration

Concentration is mass divided by volume. A vial containing 10 mg reconstituted in 2 mL gives 5 mg/mL. Record the date, diluent and final concentration on the label.

After reconstitution

Reconstituted material is generally less stable than powder. Store it as your protocol directs and discard it by the date you set.
